# 30-Day Structured Problem Decomposition Training

A self-study program for building durable *judgment* about how to structure ambiguous
problems — not just rote MECE, but knowing which lens fits a problem, spotting overlaps and
gaps, and carrying a structure all the way to an executive-ready recommendation.

---

## What this is (and isn't)

This is a **training gym, not a documentation system.** The files exist only to force
repeated, repaired, feedback-rich reps on live ambiguous problems.

- **Is:** a 30-day, ~1 hr/day curriculum. Each day is a short lesson + a *doing* exercise +
  a self-check + a repair. You produce real artifacts, not notes.
- **Isn't:** a reading packet. Reading the lessons without doing — and *repairing* — the
  deliverables builds zero skill. The value is in the reps.

The goal: when a fuzzy problem lands on you, you don't jump to a favorite framework. You
reframe it, choose or *sequence* a useful lens, build a structure, test it, repair it, and
connect it to a decision.

---

## How to use it

**The daily path (do this every day):**
1. Open the day file.
2. Do the **Minimum Viable Rep** (25 min) — the floor for a day that "counts": produce a
   structure, score it, name one defect, repair one branch, and write one sentence on why the
   structure helps.
3. Use only the *linked* reference section if you need it — **do not read the support files
   straight through.** They're a shelf you pull from, not a textbook you read cover to cover.
4. Compare against `Calibration-Gallery.md` when the day links to it.
5. **Repair** your artifact based on the rubric and calibration.

**Other practices:**
- **Do Week 1 in order** (foundations build). After Week 1, days are mostly independent.
- **Keep your work.** You'll compare Day-30 work against your Day-1 pre-test, so save early
  attempts. (Day 1 has a pre-test built for exactly this.)
- **On checkpoint days (7, 14, 21, 26, 30):** do the **External / AI Review**, **Failure
  Diary**, and **Live-Problem Rep** built into those files. Self-grading has a blind spot;
  outside feedback is how your self-scores become trustworthy.

### Time budget

- **Minimum Viable Rep floor:** 25 min — any day "counts" if you do at least this.
- **Core:** ~60 min/day. **Stretch:** +30–45 min (optional, marked each day).
- **Gaps are fine.** After Week 1 the program tolerates missed days — just resume.

## Assumptions about you (so you can correct them)

1. **No formal consulting/MBA training**, but a strong systems thinker. The program moves
   briskly past "why structure matters" and spends the time on judgment.
2. **Self-directed and honest in self-assessment** — rubrics are self-graded.
3. **Comfortable in markdown / pen-and-paper.** No special software; trees are indented text.
4. **You have real domains to mine for cases** (studio/teaching, music production, IT
   consulting, systems architecture, outreach, a board role).
5. **~1 hr/day core, +30–45 min optional.** Tolerates gaps.
6. **Goal is internalized judgment**, not a certificate.

**If assumption #1 is wrong** (you've had consulting exposure): skim Week 1, and spend the
freed hours on the Week-3 lens-selection drills (Days 15–21) — that's where the real skill is.

|---|---|

| 2 | MECE from zero |
| 3 | The four MECE cut types (algebraic, process, segment, framework) |
| 4 | Issue trees / logic trees |
| 5 | Testing a structure: overlaps, gaps & false categories — **and repair** *(load-bearing · correction loop)* |
| 6 | Beyond MECE: usefulness & decision-relevance |
| 7 | Week 1 integration + checkpoint *(external review · failure diary · live-problem rep)* |

### Week 2 — Practice across problem types
| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| 8 | Profit / financial decomposition (the workhorse) |
| 9 | Customer / market segmentation |
| 10 | Process decomposition + process mapping |
| 11 | Root-cause: 5 Whys + fishbone |
| 12 | Everyday decision decomposition |
| 13 | Strategic decomposition (where-to-play / how-to-win) |
| 14 | Week 2 integration + checkpoint + **bridge to Week 3** *(load-bearing · correction loop · external review)* |

### Week 3 — Lens Selection **and Sequencing** (the spine)
*One recurring rich case — "ECTango class attendance is inconsistent" — re-structured through a
new lens each day so you feel what each reveals and hides, plus a fresh transfer case each day.
The skill this week is no longer "can you use the tool?" but "which tool first, and when do you
switch?"*
| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| 15 | Hypothesis-driven analysis & hypothesis trees (+ Week-2 bridge exercise) |
| 16 | When MECE is the WRONG lens |
| 17 | Systems thinking & causal-loop maps |
| 18 | Process map vs. causal/driver analysis *(load-bearing)* |
| 19 | Decision trees & expected value under uncertainty |
| 20 | Stakeholder mapping & incentives (categories vs. incentives) |
| 21 | **Lens-selection & sequencing checkpoint — the midterm** *(load-bearing · the crux · correction loop · external review)* |

### Week 4 — Synthesis, communication, capstone
| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| 22 | From decomposition to analysis plan |
| 23 | Synthesis & the Pyramid Principle |
| 24 | Executive communication (answer-first, SCQA) |
| 25 | Storylining & action titles |
| 26 | Pressure-testing + first-principles teardown *(load-bearing · correction loop · external review)* |
| 27 | Capstone pt.1: choose & justify a structure |
| 28 | Capstone pt.2: analyze & synthesize |
| 29 | Capstone pt.3: the consulting-style report |
| 30 | **Capstone review + Day-1 diagnostic comparison + cold mini-capstone + playbook** *(load-bearing · external review)* |

## Role adaptations (optional — weight these days more heavily)

- **Business analyst:** lean into Days 10, 22 (analysis plans, requirements/process
  decomposition). Your edge is rigor; push on lens-selection (Week 3) to avoid
  defaulting to MECE for everything.
- **Product manager:** Days 9, 13, 19 (segment cuts, opportunity trees, prioritization under
  uncertainty). Practice opportunity trees as a variant of the issue tree.
- **Founder:** Days 15, 19, 13 (hypothesis-first, decision trees under uncertainty,
  resource-constrained scoping). You rarely have time for full MECE — hypothesis trees are
  your default.
- **Strategy consultant:** Days 23–25, 29 (storylining, pyramid synthesis, client-ready
  summaries). The structuring is your bread and butter; invest in the synthesis/communication
  back half.

---

## After the 30 days: the repetition loop

Skill fades without reps. The Day-30 playbook sets up a weekly habit: **once a week, take one
live fuzzy problem and produce a one-page structure + recommendation** (15–20 min). Pull
problems from `Case-Bank.md` or real life. That's how this becomes reflex.

---

## Authoring checklist (for whoever edits this curriculum later)

Keep these invariants so quality stays even across all 38 files:
- Every day requires **doing**, not just reading — there is always a concrete deliverable
  with a Definition of Done.
- No lesson over-explains; concept sections stay ~150–300 words, one idea each.
- Week 1 is beginner-safe but never patronizing.
- Week 3 has enough repetition (the recurring case) to build reflex.
- Cross-links stay sparse and purposeful — point to the Field Manual / Templates / Gallery,
  don't create link noise.
- From Day 2 on, one rubric item always probes evidence ("what would show this is
  incomplete, overlapping, or decision-irrelevant?").
- Every day serves one of: lens-selection, structure quality, evidence discipline, or
  synthesis. If a day doesn't, shorten or cut it.
- No placeholders, no generic filler, no unexplained jargon. It should read as **one
  coherent curriculum**, not 38 disconnected notes.
