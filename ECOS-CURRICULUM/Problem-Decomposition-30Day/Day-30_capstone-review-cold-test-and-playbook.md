# Day 30 — Capstone Review, Cold Test & Playbook

> Week 4 · Synthesis & Transfer · *load-bearing · final day*

## Learning objective

Prove transfer: review your capstone, measure your Day-1→Day-30 growth diagnostically, pass a
cold mini-capstone under constraint, and set up the habit that keeps the skill alive.

## Minimum Viable Rep — 25 minutes
1. Produce the structure (the cold mini-capstone, Part 3 — do this first, before reviewing
   anything).
2. Score it using the rubric.
3. Name one defect.
4. Repair one branch.
5. Write one sentence: "This structure helps because…"

## Concept lesson

A capstone you spent three days polishing can be gamed; a cold problem under time pressure
cannot. Today has three parts, and the order matters — do the **cold test first**, while you
can't lean on notes, then review. The point of the whole program was never to produce pretty
diagrams; it was to change what you reach for when a fuzzy problem lands. Today measures whether
that changed.

## Part 1 — Capstone review

Score your Days 27–29 capstone against the rubric in `Capstone.md`. Be specific about where lens
choice, synthesis, or pressure-testing was strongest and weakest.

## Part 2 — Day-1 diagnostic comparison

Pull out your **Day-1 pre-test** attempts (the three ambiguous problems you saved). Redo them
now, fresh. Lay old beside new and score each on this 1–4 scale:

1. **Unstructured reaction** — jumps to solutions; no frame, no evidence standard.
2. **Basic structure** — visible categories, but weak lens choice and weak decision-relevance.
3. **Useful structure** — clear frame, appropriate lens, evidence needs named, connected to a
   decision.
4. **Judgment-ready** — justified lens *sequence*; names what the lens hides; identifies
   disconfirming evidence; produces a testable recommendation.

Name the *specific* change between Day 1 and Day 30 — not "I got better," but "Day 1 I
brainstormed a flat list; Day 30 I chose a lens on purpose, checked MECE, pruned to
decision-relevant branches, and named a switch condition."

## Part 3 — Cold mini-capstone (the transfer test)

Pick an unfamiliar problem (have a peer hand you one, or take an untouched `Case-Bank.md` case).
**30 minutes, no notes, no reference files.** Produce:
- A chosen starting lens or lens sequence.
- A 2-level structure.
- A short recommendation.
- What the structure *hides*.
- What evidence would change the answer.

This is the real exam. If you can do this cold, the skill transferred.

## Deliverable + Definition of Done

Capstone review + scored Day-1/Day-30 comparison + completed cold mini-capstone + finalized
personal cheat sheet + a scheduled repetition loop.

**Definition of Done:** the diagnostic comparison is *diagnostic* (named, scored differences —
not motivational); the cold mini-capstone was done under the 30-min/no-notes constraint; and you
have a concrete weekly rep on your calendar.

## Self-check rubric

- [ ] My Day-1/Day-30 comparison names a *specific* behavioral change, with 1–4 scores.
- [ ] The cold mini-capstone was genuinely cold (no notes, timed).
- [ ] In the cold test I chose a lens *on purpose* and named what it hides.
- [ ] **In the cold test, did I name what evidence would change my answer?** (If not, that's
      your next growth edge.)
- [ ] I scheduled the weekly repetition loop.

## Finalize your cheat sheet

Fill in the bottom of `Lens-Selection-Cheat-Sheet.md`: the lens you over-reach for, the one you
under-use, your most common decomposition mistake, and your go-to sequence for a fuzzy work
problem.

## The repetition loop (so this survives)

Schedule **one 15–20 min rep per week**: take a live fuzzy problem from a real domain and produce
a one-page structure + recommendation. Pull from `Case-Bank.md` or real life. Skill fades
without reps; this is what makes the 30 days permanent. Re-score the master end-state rubric
(`Progress-Checkpoints.md`) in 30 days.

## Reflection question

What will you reach for *first* next time a genuinely ambiguous problem lands — and how is that
different from what you'd have reached for on Day 1?
