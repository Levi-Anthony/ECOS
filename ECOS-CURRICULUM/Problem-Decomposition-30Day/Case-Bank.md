# Case Bank

Reusable, real-life problem **prompts** drawn from your domains. These are not topics — each is
a small case with a decision-maker, a tension, messy detail, and a constraint that stops "just
gather more data" from being the answer. Several are deliberately multi-lens (that's the point
of Week 3). Pull from here whenever a day says "use one of your own problems," and keep using
them after Day 30 for the weekly repetition loop.

**Case numbers are stable** — daily files cross-reference them (e.g., "Case #9"). Don't renumber.

> Tags: **MECE** issue tree · **DRIVER** algebraic tree · **SEG** segmentation · **PROC**
> process/funnel · **CAUSE** 5-Whys/fishbone · **LOOP** causal-loop · **DEC** decision tree ·
> **HYP** hypothesis tree · **STAKE** stakeholder/incentive · **FP** first-principles

## Tango studio / ECTango pedagogy

1. **Inconsistent class attendance.** *(LOOP, PROC, STAKE, HYP — the Week-3 spine case.)*
   Levi must give the venue a predictable weekly headcount. Beginner sign-ups are up after
   social posts, but repeat attendance is down and advanced dancers say energy is lower.
   Instructors blame schedule fatigue; Levi suspects curriculum sequencing. He can't run a
   months-long study — the venue wants a plan now.
2. **Beginners don't convert to regulars.** *(PROC, SEG)* Levi decides where to spend limited
   teaching energy. Lots of people try one class and never return; a few become devoted. He
   can't interview everyone who vanished. Where in the journey is the leak?
3. **Add a second beginner track or deepen the existing one?** *(DEC)* One free evening slot,
   one decision, uncertain demand either way. Committing to a new track is hard to walk back
   mid-season without looking flaky to students.
4. **Students plateau at the "intermediate wall."** *(CAUSE, FP)* Levi (as head instructor)
   wants more students reaching the milonga floor. Many stall for months. Is it teaching
   method, practice habits, or the milonga-entry barrier itself? "Just teach more" hasn't worked.
5. **Pricing the workshop series.** *(DRIVER, DEC)* Levi sets the price for a 6-week intensive.
   Revenue = seats × price × sessions; raise price and fill fewer seats, or fill the room at
   thinner margin. He has one shot at announcing the price.

## Music production

6. **Tracks take too long to finish.** *(PROC, CAUSE)* Levi wants to release more consistently.
   Songs stall — but at different stages (writing, arrangement, mixing, the "is it done?" loop).
   He can't tell if it's one bottleneck or many. Hiring out mixing is expensive and may not be
   the real issue.
7. **Singles drip vs. full EP?** *(DEC, STAKE)* One body of finished work, one release decision.
   Audience and platform behavior are uncertain, and the choice shapes months of promotion he
   can't easily redo.
8. **The mix sounds thin on small speakers.** *(CAUSE, FP)* A specific technical defect with a
   deadline. Is it the arrangement, the mix, or the master? Endlessly tweaking plugins hasn't
   fixed it; he needs to find the actual cause.

## IT consulting

9. **A client's network slows every afternoon.** *(CAUSE, PROC)* The client's office manager is
   losing patience. It's reproducible (~2pm daily) but the cause isn't obvious. The client won't
   pay for a blank-check investigation — Levi needs to localize it fast.
10. **Productize a fixed-scope support offering vs. stay hourly?** *(DEC, DRIVER)* Levi decides
    his business model for next year under uncertain demand. Switching pricing models is
    disruptive to existing clients and hard to reverse once announced.
11. **Clients churn after the first 90 days.** *(PROC, SEG, LOOP)* Levi wants to stop the
    bleed but can't survey churned clients (they've left). Is it onboarding, a particular client
    type, or a trust spiral where early friction compounds? *(Rich multi-lens case.)*
12. **Scope creep keeps eating margins.** *(CAUSE, STAKE)* Levi keeps finishing projects over
    budget. Clients, Levi, and the contract structure all push toward creep. Just saying "no
    more" risks the client relationship.

## ECOS / BRAIN architecture

13. **Boot context sometimes feels thin / continuity gaps.** *(MECE, CAUSE)* Levi (as architect)
    decides where to invest next. Continuity occasionally fails, but he can't tell if it's
    capture, retrieval, or snapshot quality. A full rebuild is too costly to do on a hunch.
14. **Make capture more automatic, or keep it propose-first?** *(DEC, STAKE)* A design
    trade-off between friction and control. More automation risks polluting BRAIN; more control
    risks lost captures. The choice sets a hard-to-reverse default.

## Neil book outreach

15. **An outreach wave gets low response.** *(PROC, HYP, SEG)* Levi must decide whether to fix
    the message, the targeting, or the list before the next wave. "The copy is weak" is the
    obvious story — but is it? He has limited contacts and can't burn the list testing endlessly.

## TTC board

16. **Festival attendance / volunteer turnout declining.** *(LOOP, STAKE, MECE)* The board needs
    a direction for next year's festival. Decline looks like a slow spiral, and volunteers,
    attendees, and organizers all have different incentives. Big format changes are public and
    hard to undo.
17. **Change the festival format?** *(DEC, STAKE)* A high-stakes board decision with many
    interested parties and a reputation cost if it flops. Doing nothing is also a choice.

## How to pick a case for a given day

- **Clean number to break down?** → 5, 10, 11, 13 (DRIVER).
- **A sequence/funnel?** → 2, 6, 9, 11, 15 (PROC).
- **A feedback loop?** → 1, 11, 16 (LOOP).
- **A real decision?** → 3, 7, 10, 14, 17 (DEC).
- **A root-cause hunt?** → 4, 6, 8, 9, 12 (CAUSE).
- **A people/incentives problem?** → 12, 14, 16, 17 (STAKE).
- **The hardest, multi-lens cases (good capstone candidates)?** → 1, 11, 16.
