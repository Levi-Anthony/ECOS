# Day 24 — Executive Communication: Answer-First & SCQA

> Week 4 · Synthesis & Communication

## Learning objective

Write a recommendation that **leads with the answer**, frames *why it matters* with SCQA, and
fits on one page — and that names a **testable recommendation with a reason**, not "keep
monitoring."

## Minimum Viable Rep — 25 minutes
1. Produce the structure (a Template-6 answer-first executive summary for the attendance case).
2. Score it using the rubric.
3. Name one defect.
4. Repair one branch.
5. Write one sentence: "This structure helps because…"

## Concept lesson

A busy decision-maker reads the first sentence and decides whether to keep reading. So **put the
answer first** — the recommendation, in one sentence, before the reasoning. This inverts how you
*did* the work (evidence → conclusion) into how you *communicate* it (conclusion → evidence). The
reasoning still comes; it just comes second, as support a skeptic can audit.

**SCQA** frames why the answer matters: **S**ituation (the stable context everyone agrees on),
**C**omplication (what changed / the tension), **Q**uestion (the decision that raises), **A**nswer
(your recommendation). SCQA earns the reader's attention before you spend it; answer-first respects
it once you have it. Template 6 stacks both: SCQA frame on top, then the pyramid (one message, three
supports) underneath.

The non-negotiable standard for Week 4: **a recommendation is a testable claim with a reason, not
a holding pattern.**

> **BAD:** "Attendance is concerning. We should keep monitoring the 6pm slot and gather more data
> before deciding." *(No decision. No reason. No way to be wrong. This is consulting theater —
> motion that looks like progress.)*
>
> **GOOD:** "Move the 6pm beginner class to 7:30pm for a 4-week trial, because the data shows the
> slot fails on schedule fit, not teaching quality — and the trial is cheap and reversible. If
> 7:30pm attendance doesn't beat 6pm by week 3, we revert." *(A decision, a reason, a kill
> condition.)*

If your "answer" could be the answer to *any* problem ("monitor, align, gather data"), you haven't
answered. Name the move and the reason it's right.

## Practice case

Decision-maker: **you, drafting the one-page case you'd actually act on for the 6pm slot** (and
could show a co-teacher or the studio owner). You hold the Day-23 synthesis: *schedule fit, not
quality, is the constraint.* Write it answer-first.

The trap: you don't have perfect data — first-visit-to-return is confirmed, but you haven't proven
a time change *recovers* attendance. The cowardly move is to recommend "more analysis." The
constraint that blocks it: **the term schedule locks in two weeks**; not deciding *is* a decision
to keep 6pm. Recommend a testable action anyway, with the kill condition built in.

## Deliverable + Definition of Done

A one-page Template-6 summary: SCQA frame, then a one-sentence answer-first recommendation, then 3
supports with evidence, then the next step.

**Definition of Done:** a reader who stops after the answer sentence still knows what you
recommend and the single biggest reason; the recommendation is a *testable action with a reason
and a kill condition*, not "monitor/gather data"; and it names the **irreversible/reputation-
sensitive action it triggers** — committing the printed term schedule, which students plan their
lives around.

## Self-check rubric

- [ ] The recommendation appears as ONE sentence before any reasoning.
- [ ] SCQA frames why the decision matters (not just background).
- [ ] The recommendation is a testable action with a reason — NOT "keep monitoring / gather data."
- [ ] It carries a kill condition (what would make me revert or be wrong).
- [ ] **What evidence, example, or counterexample would show this is incomplete, overlapping, or decision-irrelevant?** (e.g., the answer fits any problem, so it answers none.)
- [ ] I named the irreversible/reputation-sensitive action this triggers.

## Common mistake to watch for

**The hedge disguised as rigor.** "More study needed before we commit" feels responsible but is
usually fear of being wrong wearing a lab coat. If a decision must be made on a deadline, "gather
more data" is not neutral — it's choosing the status quo by default. Recommend, with a reason and
a way to be proven wrong.

## When to use / when NOT to use

**Use** answer-first for any recommendation to someone whose time or trust is finite — owners,
clients, the TTC board. **Don't** lead with the answer in a genuine *exploration* where the
audience asked you to map options, not pick one; there, SCQA-then-options is honest. The tell is
whether they want a decision or a tour.

## Reflection question

Read your answer sentence alone. Could it be the conclusion of a completely different
investigation? If yes, it's a template phrase, not your answer — rewrite it until it could only
follow *this* analysis.

## Optional stretch

Find a real recommendation you once made (an IT proposal, a release plan). Rewrite its opening as
a single answer-first sentence with a reason and a kill condition. Compare to how you originally
buried it.
