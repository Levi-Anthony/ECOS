# Day 3 — The Four MECE Cut Types

> Week 1 · Foundations

## Learning objective

Recognize the four standard ways to cut a problem MECE-ly, and feel how different cuts of the
*same* problem reveal different things.

## Minimum Viable Rep — 25 minutes
1. Produce the structure (two different cuts of the same problem, below).
2. Score it using the rubric.
3. Name one defect.
4. Repair one branch.
5. Write one sentence: "This structure helps because…"

## Concept lesson

There isn't one "right" MECE breakdown — there are families of cuts. Knowing the four standard
types means you always have a starting move:

1. **Algebraic / formula:** break a quantity into factors that combine to make it. *Profit =
   Revenue − Cost. Revenue = Price × Volume.* Best when the thing you care about is a number.
2. **Process / stage:** split by the steps something passes through, in order. *Awareness →
   trial → purchase → repeat.* Best when the problem lives in a sequence.
3. **Segmentation / attribute:** split a population by a characteristic. *By customer type, by
   region, by product line.* Best when groups behave differently.
4. **Conceptual framework:** use an established model as the cut. *People/Process/Technology;
   the 4 Ps; Strengths/Weaknesses/Opportunities/Threats.* Best when a proven framework fits
   and saves you reinventing the wheel.

The deeper lesson: **the cut you choose decides what you'll see.** Cut "grow revenue"
algebraically and you see pricing and volume levers; cut it by segment and you see *which
customers* to chase. Neither is wrong — they surface different opportunities. Strong thinkers
choose the cut on purpose, knowing what it will and won't reveal.

## Practice case

Take one problem — **"grow studio revenue"** (or a revenue problem from your own work) — and
build **two different MECE cuts** of it: one algebraic, one by segment. Write one sentence on
what each cut reveals that the other hides.

## Deliverable + Definition of Done

Two MECE breakdowns of the same problem, plus a one-sentence "reveals/hides" note for each.

**Definition of Done:** both cuts are individually MECE, they use genuinely *different*
organizing principles, and you've named a specific lever or insight each one surfaces that the
other doesn't.

## Self-check rubric

- [ ] Cut 1 is algebraic and MECE.
- [ ] Cut 2 is a segmentation and MECE.
- [ ] The two cuts are genuinely different, not the same cut relabeled.
- [ ] **What evidence, example, or counterexample would show a cut is incomplete, overlapping,
      or decision-irrelevant — and what does each cut cause me to *miss*?** (Naming the blind
      spot is the point.)

## Common mistake to watch for

Building "two cuts" that are really the same cut in different words. If both trees lead you to
the same first move, you only made one cut. Push for cuts that point at *different* levers.

## When to use / when NOT to use

**Use** the four types as your menu whenever you need a MECE breakdown and aren't sure where
to start. **Don't** force a framework cut (type 4) onto a problem just because the framework
is famous — a borrowed model that doesn't fit is worse than a simple algebraic cut.

## Reflection question

Which of the four cut types do you reach for naturally, and which do you neglect? Your blind
spot is probably a cut type you never try.

## Optional stretch

Cut the same problem a *third* way (process or framework). With three cuts in front of you,
which would you actually pursue first, and why?
