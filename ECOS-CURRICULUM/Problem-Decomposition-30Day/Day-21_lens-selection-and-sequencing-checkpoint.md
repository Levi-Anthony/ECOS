# Day 21 — Lens Selection & Sequencing Checkpoint (Midterm)

> Week 3 · Lens Selection & Sequencing · *load-bearing · checkpoint · correction loop · the crux*

## Learning objective

Given a fuzzy problem, pick a starting lens *with a reason*, name what it reveals and hides, and
**sequence** to a second lens when the first stops paying off — using the diagnostic questions,
not reflex.

## Minimum Viable Rep — 25 minutes
1. Produce the structure (work Case 4 — the misleading one — end to end).
2. Score it using the rubric.
3. Name one defect.
4. Repair one branch.
5. Write one sentence: "This structure helps because…"

## Concept lesson

This is the day the whole program builds toward. You've met the lenses; now you choose among
them under ambiguity. Two truths to hold:

1. **Lens choice is justified by features of the problem.** Run the diagnostic questions (Field
   Manual §3): Am I explaining or choosing? Is there feedback/circular causality? Real
   uncertainty with payoffs? A cleanly enumerable space? A hunch worth testing fast? Each strong
   "yes" points to a lens.
2. **The answer is often a *sequence*, not a single lens.** Real judgment looks like: "Started
   with a process map because it looked like a bottleneck; the map showed the bottleneck was
   *behavioral*, not mechanical, so I switched to an incentive map; that revealed organizers are
   rewarded for attendance *volume*, not *repeat* attendance; my next test is a retention
   intervention." Naming the *switch condition* is the skill.

The misleading case (Case 4 below) exists to break the reflex of matching surface symptoms to a
tool. Its surface suggests one lens; its root needs another.

## Practice case — four problems

For each: state the diagnostic answers, name the starting lens, what it reveals/hides, and (where
needed) the switch condition and second lens.
1. **Clean / enumerable:** "List and size every way the studio could cut costs next year."
2. **Causal / systems:** "Turnout, room energy, and retention all seem to drag each other down
   month over month."
3. **Decision under uncertainty:** "Sign a 12-month venue lease now, or stay month-to-month at a
   higher rate?"
4. **Misleading:** "Our Neil-outreach wave got almost no replies, so the email copy must be
   weak." *(Surface says 'fix the copy' — a content problem. Is it? Where might the real leak
   be, and which lens finds it?)*

## Deliverable + Definition of Done

For all four: diagnostic answers + starting lens + reveals/hides; for Cases 2 and 4, a switch
condition and a named second lens.

**Definition of Done:** no case is answered with a reflexive single tool; Case 4's answer
*resists* the surface framing and relocates the problem; and every choice names the decision it
improves. If all four felt like obvious one-tool problems, you misread at least one — re-examine
Case 4.

## Self-check rubric

- [ ] Each lens choice is tied to specific diagnostic answers, not habit.
- [ ] For ≥2 cases I named a switch condition and a plausible second lens.
- [ ] On Case 4 I challenged the surface framing instead of accepting "fix the copy."
- [ ] **For my hardest case, what evidence would show I chose the wrong starting lens?** (Name it.)
- [ ] I can state the decision or next test each structure improves.

## Common mistake to watch for

**Symptom-matching to a tool.** "It's about people → stakeholder map; it's a number → driver
tree" — applied without checking whether the surface symptom is the real problem. Case 4
punishes exactly this: the obvious "content" framing hides a likely targeting/funnel leak.

## When to use / when NOT to use

**Use** the diagnostic questions whenever a problem doesn't announce its own shape. **Don't**
treat the cheat sheet as a lookup table that hands you one answer — it's a *judgment aid* that
should leave you holding a starting lens and a switch condition, not a single verdict.

## External / AI Review
Paste a case answer (no private details) into an AI or show a peer. Ask for:
1. Overlap/gap check. 2. Decision-relevance check. 3. Hidden-assumption check. 4. One stronger
alternative structure. 5. What evidence would change the recommendation. 6. Useful or merely tidy?
Specifically ask: *did I accept Case 4's surface framing too easily?*
