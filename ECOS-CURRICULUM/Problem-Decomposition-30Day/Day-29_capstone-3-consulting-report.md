# Day 29 — Capstone pt.3: The Consulting-Style Report

> Week 4 · Capstone

## Learning objective

Compress the whole capstone into a **one-page report**: an answer-first executive summary (SCQA +
pyramid), a trimmed supporting structure, action-title headings, and an honest risks / "what would
have to be true" section — such that a reader who sees only the first paragraph still gets your
recommendation and its top reason. This is Day 29 of the brief in `Capstone.md`; work that file as
your spec.

## Minimum Viable Rep — 25 minutes
1. Produce the structure (the one-page consulting-style report for your capstone).
2. Score it using the rubric.
3. Name one defect.
4. Repair one branch.
5. Write one sentence: "This structure helps because…"

## Concept lesson

This is the assembly day — every Week-4 skill in one artifact. The report has four parts, in order:

- **Executive summary (Template 6):** SCQA frames *why it matters* (Situation / Complication /
  Question), then the **answer-first** recommendation — one sentence, before any reasoning — with
  the pyramid's three supports beneath it (Day 24).
- **Supporting structure:** the *trimmed* tree/map from Day 27, showing how you got there. Trimmed
  means dead and decision-irrelevant branches are cut; the reader sees the spine, not the scaffolding.
- **Action titles:** every section heading is a complete-sentence claim, and the headings in
  sequence tell the story (Day 25).
- **Risks / what would have to be true:** 2–3 *honest* caveats — the load-bearing assumptions and
  the specific observation that would prove you wrong (Day 26). Not generic risk theater; each
  caveat names a disconfirming test.

The whole thing is one page. The discipline isn't length — it's that **the recommendation is a
testable action with a reason, not "keep monitoring."** A capstone report that ends on "we should
gather more data and revisit" has failed: it spent three days building structure to avoid making a
call. Name the move, the reason, and the kill condition.

## Practice case

Decision-maker: **the real recipient of your capstone recommendation** — the TTC board, an IT
client, the studio, or yourself acting on an ECOS/BRAIN architecture decision. Take your Day-28
governing message and verdicts and produce the one-page report.

The trap and the constraint: a one-page limit forces you to *cut*, and the cowardly cut keeps all
the analysis and drops the recommendation's spine. The `Capstone.md` Definition of Done forbids
hiding behind volume: **a reader who sees only the first paragraph must still get the recommendation
and the top reason.** Whatever decision hangs on this — and its deadline — is what makes "more
study" unavailable; the page has to commit.

## Deliverable + Definition of Done

Per the `Capstone.md` Day-29 spec and model-answer outline: a one-page report with exec summary
(SCQA + answer-first, Template 6), trimmed supporting structure, action-title headings, and a
risks / "what would have to be true" section (2–3 caveats with disconfirming tests, from Day 26).

**Definition of Done:** a reader who sees only the first paragraph still gets your recommendation
and the top reason; the recommendation is a *testable action with a reason and a kill condition*,
not "keep monitoring"; the risks name disconfirming tests, not generic worries; and the report
names the **irreversible/reputation-sensitive action it asks for** — the actual board vote, client
sign-off, schedule commit, or architecture change you're recommending, which is the next move that
can't be quietly undone.

## Self-check rubric

- [ ] A reader who stops after the first paragraph still gets the recommendation and top reason.
- [ ] The exec summary is SCQA + answer-first (Template 6); the answer is one sentence, up top.
- [ ] Every heading is an action-title claim; in sequence they tell the story (Day 25).
- [ ] Risks name load-bearing assumptions with disconfirming tests, not generic worries (Day 26).
- [ ] **What evidence, example, or counterexample would show this is incomplete, overlapping, or decision-irrelevant?** (e.g., the recommendation could conclude any investigation.)
- [ ] I named the irreversible/reputation-sensitive action the report asks for.

## Common mistake to watch for

**Burying the answer to seem thorough.** Leading with situation, structure, and analysis so the
recommendation arrives on the last line — where a busy reader never reaches it. Thoroughness is the
support *beneath* the answer, not a wall *in front* of it. If the first paragraph doesn't carry the
verdict, the report has failed its single Definition-of-Done test.

## When to use / when NOT to use

**Use** this one-page answer-first format for any real recommendation to a decision-maker whose
time and trust are finite — boards, clients, owners. **Don't** use it when the audience explicitly
asked you to *map options* rather than pick one; there, SCQA-then-options is the honest shape (Day
24). The capstone, by design, asks you to pick.

## Reflection question

Read your first paragraph alone, then your risks section alone. Do they describe the *same*
recommendation honestly — a real call with real caveats — or did you write a confident summary on
top of a hedge? The gap between the two is where a reader loses trust.

## Optional stretch

Hand the one-page report (or just its first paragraph) to someone outside the problem. Ask only:
"What am I recommending, and why?" If they can answer from the first paragraph, you passed the
Definition of Done. If they have to read the whole page, find where the answer got buried and lift
it up.
