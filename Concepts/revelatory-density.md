# Revelatory Density

**Revelatory density** is the degree to which a concrete instance exposes the underlying abstract structure of its domain per unit of examination. It measures how efficiently studying a case yields generalizable insight.

A paradigm has high revelatory density: examining it efficiently extracts the abstract pattern it exemplifies. A bare instance has low revelatory density: it belongs to the pattern without disclosing it. The concept is closely related to but distinct from *inferential reach* (how far generalization is licensed) and *structural transparency* (how directly the instance encodes the pattern). Together these three form the organizing axis of any epistemic hierarchy of examples.

Revelatory density connects to the mathematical notion of **canonical form** — not the most general expression of a structure, but the most *revealing* one. Both are specific, concrete things that maximize structural legibility. The concept also has a natural information-theoretic shadow: it is roughly the mutual information between a case and the abstract structure it belongs to, normalized by the effort of examination — though this formalization remains an open thread.
