# Epistemic Hierarchy

An **epistemic hierarchy** is a hierarchy organized around **epistemic warrant** — how much a given level licenses generalization and theory-building. Higher levels are defined as distillations of lower ones: they have survived more scrutiny, compressed more evidence, and carry more inferential reach. The example-typicality hierarchy (paradigm → prototype → exemplar → specimen → instance) is an epistemic hierarchy.

In epistemic hierarchies, **authority and abstraction tend to co-vary** — but this is a domain-scoped correlation, not a structural law. It holds specifically because higher levels are *earned by* surviving examination of lower ones. The correlation breaks in at least four cases: (1) concrete sovereigns (authority without abstraction — a king's command); (2) vacuous superordinates (abstraction without authority — "thing," "entity"); (3) part-whole hierarchies (the direction of the correlation depends on which question is being asked); (4) cognitive vs. epistemic authority (the prototype has more cognitive authority than the paradigm; the paradigm has more epistemic authority).

The primary axis of an epistemic hierarchy is best labeled **revelatory density**, **inferential reach**, or **structural transparency** — not "authority," which imports a social framing that obscures the epistemic mechanism.
