# Ontological Priority and Grounding

**Ontological priority** is the philosophical term for the relation in which one thing must exist before or in order for another to exist. It organizes hierarchies around *dependence* rather than abstraction or typicality. Atoms have ontological priority over molecules; foundations have ontological priority over the buildings they support.

**Grounding** (in contemporary analytic metaphysics, associated with Kit Fine, Jonathan Schaffer, and others) is a stronger and more specific claim: one thing *explains the being* of another, not merely precedes it causally or temporally. A molecule is grounded in its constituent atoms — it exists *because of* and *through* them. Grounding is a non-causal, explanatory relation between levels of reality.

**Emergence** is the complementary upward-facing phenomenon: higher levels acquire properties genuinely absent from lower ones. Water has properties (liquidity, surface tension) that neither hydrogen nor oxygen atoms have. Emergence means the hierarchy does not simply reduce in either direction — each level has irreducible character.

Together, these three concepts organize **constitutive hierarchies** — hierarchies where the question is not "what kind of thing is this?" (taxonomy) or "how typical is this?" (prototype theory) but "what makes this thing exist and what does it give rise to?"

The theological case is a philosophically interesting exception: in classical theism, God has ontological priority over creation but is not *constituted by* creation. The constitutive direction is inverted — priority runs downward while constitution does not run upward. This structure may have formal analogs in attractors (dynamical systems) and fixed points (mathematics), though this remains an open thread.
