# Paradigm vs Prototype

**Paradigm** and **prototype** are both "gold-star" instances of their categories, but they earn that status through entirely different mechanisms and occupy different structural positions.

A **paradigm** is epistemically privileged: it has survived institutional and critical scrutiny, licenses broad generalization, and defines how a community of practice understands a concept. Its position is *prescribed* — the field designates it. A **prototype** is cognitively privileged: it is the most statistically central member of a category, the instance that shares the most attributes with other members and fewest with non-members. Its position is *described* — it emerges from the distribution of instances. Rosch's theory reveals these as operating on different axes: paradigm is a claim about *vertical* position in a hierarchy (the epistemically privileged tier), while prototype is a claim about *horizontal* position (the most typical member within a tier). A five-level ranking that places them adjacently treats them as the same kind of thing; they are not.

The confusion is productive: both maximize something like **cue validity** — richest attribute cluster per cognitive effort — but the "effort" being minimized is different in each case. For prototypes it is perceptual-cognitive effort; for paradigms it is the effort of epistemic validation.
