# Basic Level Category

The **basic level category** is Eleanor Rosch's term for the cognitively privileged tier in a taxonomic hierarchy — the level acquired first by children, processed fastest by adults, and informationally richest relative to cognitive effort. In the hierarchy *furniture → chair → kitchen chair*, "chair" is the basic level.

The basic level is defined by maximizing **cue validity**: it is the tier where attribute clusters are densest and most distinctive. Superordinate levels (furniture) are too broad — members share few attributes with each other. Subordinate levels (kitchen chair) are too narrow — they add little new information. The basic level sits at the sweet spot.

Crucially, the basic level term tends to **colonize the domain**: "chair" can stand in informally for the whole furniture category in a way that "furniture" and "kitchen chair" cannot. This makes it closely related to but distinct from the **unmarked term** — the basic level is a cognitive claim, the unmarked term is a linguistic claim. A word can be both simultaneously. "Example" in the example-typicality hierarchy is both: it is the basic level (natural granularity of the domain) and the unmarked term (no restrictive feature specification).
