Control (stable-for-now) — One-page report

  

  

Subtype list + one-sentence descriptions

  

1. Support & Axis Governance — Maintaining organized support under load so transfers and transitions happen cleanly without tipping, grabbing, or bailout steps.
2. Spatial Geometry Authority — Placing the body/feet where intended while governing path, distance/angle, facing, and pivot size so the movement’s geometry is deliberate.
3. Temporal Placement & Containment — Choosing when actions happen and keeping actions contained to the chosen rhythmic frame (no rushing, anticipations, or leakage).
4. Dynamic Modulation — Regulating tone and force so attack/softness/impact are intentional rather than braced, limp, jolty, or smeared.
5. Coordination Architecture — Building the action with correct internal organization (segmentation, sourcing, sequencing) and being able to steer mid-action without needing a full reset.
6. Partner Coupling Control — Regulating what you transmit through contact so pressure, frame tone, isolation, and relative position remain intentional rather than dragging, bouncing, collapsing, or grabbing.

  

  

Concerns, flags, and confidence

  

Highest-confidence separations (strong operational fork-tests):

I’m most confident in Partner Coupling Control as distinct, because “contact on/off” is a brutal separator: if the problem appears only in connection, solo mechanics work is predictably the wrong first move. I’m also high-confidence on Temporal Placement & Containment as distinct, because “in-place + metronome + leak rules” can isolate timing/containment with very low geometry and support demands. Those two have clean, repeatable “this is not that” tests.

  

Medium-confidence area: Support vs Dynamics vs Coordination.

These three interact heavily, and the main risk is “boundary blur” in real teaching: bracing can masquerade as stability; poor architecture can cause apparent stability loss; support weakness can force compensations that look like tone problems. We put “not-this” boundaries in place, but the confidence depends on whether your fork-tests are run consistently (e.g., supported dry-run to isolate architecture; tone-ladder while holding geometry constant to isolate dynamics; load-transfer pauses to isolate support). If you skip those, these will collapse back into “they just lack control,” which defeats the praxis.

  

Lowest-confidence area (explicit flag): Momentum/Perturbation.

We intentionally demoted “Momentum & Perturbation” from a subtype into a stress-test condition, but this is the most likely place for a future re-split. If you repeatedly see dancers who pass the other subtypes under low momentum yet fail catastrophically under accel/decel or perturbation, and the best first-line intervention is neither “support” nor “dynamics” nor “temporal,” then Momentum/Perturbation earns its own subtype back. Right now it’s a hypothesis, not a closed question.

  

Second explicit flag: Geometry may be over-merged.

“Spatial Geometry Authority” is merged (translation/path + rotation/orientation). That’s probably right for “few clear subtypes,” but it carries a risk: some students are accurate in endpoints yet fail facing/pivot sizing even with explicit orientation markers. If that pattern shows up reliably and needs different first-line interventions, Geometry should split into “Translation Targeting” vs “Rotation/Orientation Authority.” Until we see that anomaly repeatedly, the merge is operationally simpler.

Awareness subtypes (locked for now)

  

  

Somatic State Awareness: You can notice when your body is gripping, bracing, holding breath, or otherwise “not available,” and that state is shaping the movement.

Dynamics and Effort Awareness: You can feel how much momentum/elasticity/effort you’re carrying and whether the movement is light, heavy, jerky, or runaway.

  

Musical Awareness: You can perceive the beat and the bigger musical moments (phrases, accents, rests) clearly enough to time and shape movement with them.

  

Partner Haptic Awareness: You can feel what your partner is doing and what you’re communicating through touch—where the contact is and what direction/quality the pressure is.

  

Environment and Floorcraft Awareness: You can keep track of the room and traffic while dancing so you choose safe paths and don’t collide.

  

  

  

  

Concerns, flags, and confidence notes

  

  

The biggest structural risk is that Musical Awareness is a content bucket rather than an awareness mechanism. It survived because time-grid and phrase/salience merged cleanly under merge pressure and because the intervention family is distinct (listening/mapping tasks). But there’s still a plausible alternative model where this is simply “salience perception” with music as the target. If we later find consistent cases where a dancer can perceive music accurately yet cannot couple that perception into partnered action—and the first-line fix is not musical mapping—then Musical Awareness either splits (perception vs coupling) or gets reclassified as Control/coordination rather than Awareness.

  

The second risk is that Self Spatial Mapping Awareness is broad enough to hide a real split between “body map/segment differentiation” and “geometry/pathway planning.” The merge step said they were mergeable because the interventions overlapped heavily (tracing, tactile targets, corridor constraints), but that’s also exactly how a false merge happens: two related problems share tools. The tell would be a reliable fork: corridor/path drills fix navigation/clearance quickly while segment differentiation remains wrong and still matters (or the reverse). If that fork shows up in matched micro-cases, we should split the subtype.

  

Confidence is highest in the mechanism-heavy, teacher-robust items: Somatic State, Support/Axis, Partner Haptics, and Environment/Floorcraft. These have strong failure signatures that show up across levels, and they usually change the first intervention decisively. Confidence is moderate for Dynamics/Effort (it’s real, but it can sometimes masquerade as Support/Axis or Somatic State), and lowest for Musical Awareness and Self Spatial Mapping for the reasons above.
