## Core taxonomies and distinctions

- **Example-typicality hierarchy** (epistemic, organized by revelatory density):
  *Paradigm* (field-defining, prescribed) → *Prototype* (statistically central, described) → *Exemplar* (pedagogically strong, non-canonical) → *Specimen* (reference, inert) → *Instance* (logical, neutral) / *Example* (unmarked domain term)

- **Rosch's two dimensions:**
  *Vertical* = basic level (most cognitively privileged tier) | *Horizontal* = prototype (most typical member within a tier). Both maximize cue validity but along different axes.

- **Basic level vs. unmarked term:**
  Basic level = cognitive claim (processing speed, acquisition order) | Unmarked term = linguistic claim (absence of feature specification). A word can be both.

- **Taxonomy vs. mereology:**
  Is-a (inheritance, clean transitivity) vs. part-of (no inheritance, fragile transitivity, six subtypes per Winston et al.)

- **Epistemic hierarchy axis:**
  Not "authority" (social) but: *revelatory density* (exposure of structure), *inferential reach* (licensed generalization), *structural transparency* (encoding of pattern)

- **Constitutive hierarchy concepts:**
  *Ontological priority* (dependence), *grounding* (explanatory constitution), *emergence* (irreducible upward properties)

## Key vocabulary
- **Paradigm**: canonical, prescriptive, field-defining; high revelatory density
- **Prototype**: most statistically typical member; horizontal Roschian concept
- **Basic level**: most cognitively privileged tier; vertical Roschian concept
- **Unmarked term**: no restrictive feature specification; stands in for whole category
- **Cue validity**: richness of attribute cluster per cognitive effort
- **Revelatory density**: how much underlying structure a case exposes per unit of examination
- **Inferential reach**: how far generalization is legitimately licensed from a case
- **Structural transparency**: how directly a case encodes its abstract pattern
- **Taxonomic relation**: is-a; entails inheritance and clean transitivity
- **Meronymic relation**: part-of; no inheritance, fragile transitivity, six subtypes
- **Ontological priority**: what must exist before what; organized by dependence
- **Grounding**: explanatory constitution — one thing exists *through* another
- **Emergence**: higher levels acquire properties absent from lower ones
